## What the selected receiver costs

Let M be the actual pressure-corrected transverse polarization map along q,
with terminal covector xi*=4(n cross p). The full matching now gives

    |xi(0)|/|xi*| comparable to ell,
    |M^*p| comparable to ell^3,
    |M^*n| comparable to ell^(5/2).

A pure terminal vector n has initial-to-final strain cost |M^*p|. That
specified receiver is expensive. Constraining only its terminal normal
component to one instead has least initial leading-strain cost

    C_opt=(|xi(0)|/|xi*|)/|M^*n| comparable to ell^(-3/2).

The explicit selected branch reaches p and n components of orders ell^3
and ell^(5/2), with central history integral at most C ell^3. Normalize by
the normal component and multiply by velocity amplitude k ell^(7/8).
The resulting original strain is O(ell^(-5/8)); its final p strain and
whole-support history integral are O(ell^(11/8)), with a matching final
lower bound. Since ell^2=log(1/h), the nonlinear energy factor remains
h^(-o(1)). The proof uses that history, not a generic polynomial propagator
bound for arbitrary errors.

The receiver is already finer at time zero: its own central wavelength
increases from k/ell to order k. The theorem does not manufacture a new
frequency, and its total mixed strain already exceeds the host at t_f.
It does not add the pure late receiver's ell^(1/4) relative gain afterward.
