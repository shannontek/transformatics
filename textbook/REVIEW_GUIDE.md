# Reviewer guide

*Added 8 September 2026.*

Two kinds of reading would help this edition: a first pass from someone who
knows single-variable calculus, and a critical pass from someone who knows
analysis or PDEs. The questions below suggest what a first review should probe.
A precise stopping point is a useful result; you do not need to finish.

## If you know single-variable calculus

Read [the preface](chapters/index.md), the
[calculus bridge](chapters/calculus-bridge.md), and the first five foundation
chapters. Work the "Try it" exercises before reading their solutions.

Please note:

- the first sentence or formula you could not follow, quoted exactly;
- any place where an example, diagram or exercise did not match its explanation;
- any paragraph you skipped because it was too long or too abstract;
- anything that reads as exaggeration rather than teaching.

## If you know analysis or PDEs

Read the [reading map](chapters/reader-map.md), [the equation](chapters/equation.md),
[the published-proof chapter](chapters/published-proof.md), and the
[claim register](claims.md); then sample two research chapters that interest you.

Please check:

- whether every stated theorem has explicit hypotheses and unambiguous
  quantifiers;
- whether the forced/unforced distinction is preserved;
- whether each claim's evidence record matches its linked receipt — in
  particular, whether a written proof, an AI-assisted review and a Lean check
  are kept separate;
- whether any sentence reads as external expert acceptance, publication, or a
  solution to the unforced problem.

Report missing hypotheses, obscured hard steps and overstatements with the
chapter, section and quoted sentence.

## How to report

Send notes to the maintainer through the repository issue tracker.
For each item, give the file or page, the quoted text, what you expected and
what happened. Do not send credentials, unpublished third-party material or
personal data.

No human review has been arranged, commissioned or received.
